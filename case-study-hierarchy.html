LLM Tooling · JPMorgan Chase · Financial Sponsors · Feb – Jul 2024

An ownership-hierarchy tool relationship teams could actually query

Software Engineering Intern. Financial Sponsors covers private-equity-owned clients, whose ownership structures are deep, multi-tier, and constantly shifting. I built a tool that answers questions about them in plain language and recalculates the whole tree the instant something changes.

The problem

A private-equity sponsor's portfolio isn't a flat list — it's a tree of holding companies, sub-holdings, and operating entities, several layers deep. When one investment's value changes, that change ripples upward through every parent above it. Relationship teams needed a way to see that ripple instantly and ask questions about the structure in plain language, instead of manually tracing a spreadsheet every time something moved.

What I built

A Python tool with LLM-powered natural-language querying that visualizes multi-tier parent-child ownership structures. Change one investment's value, and the tool automatically recalculates every cascading shift up the hierarchy — giving relationship teams instant clarity on portfolio-wide impact instead of a manual re-trace.

Try it live

Click any blue-bordered leaf box to change its value, and watch every parent total above it recalculate with an animated cascade.

Why it mattered

  • Relationship teams got instant, portfolio-wide clarity on impact instead of manually re-tracing a hierarchy every time an investment changed.
  • Natural-language querying meant the tool didn't require knowing the exact structure of the underlying data to get an answer — a pattern that shows up again in the Confluence RAG assistant and migration-status chatbot described in the other case study.
